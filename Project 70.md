# Project 70: The Trans-Colorado Super-Corridor
**Document Version:** 2.0.0 (Unified I-70 Master Plan)
**Lead Architect:** Chief Engineer Flench
**Jurisdiction:** Colorado Transit Authority (Eastern & Western Slope Divisions)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Interstate 70 is the primary east-west artery of Colorado, yet it traverses two radically different geographical extremes. The eastern half spans the flat, expansive High Plains, offering virtually unlimited Right-of-Way (ROW) for high-speed rail and commercial freight innovation. The western half slams into the Rocky Mountains, creating severe bottlenecks choked by 7% grades, narrow blast-rock canyons, and extreme alpine weather.

**Project 70** unifies these two halves into a cohesive multi-modal network. It utilizes a 250-foot, 220 mph rail-and-freight super-artery for the Eastern Plains, and a surgical, hybrid infrastructure approach (tilt-trains and advanced BRT) to conquer the Western Slope and Continental Divide.

# PART I: iBetter 70 East (The High Plains Super-Corridor)
*Jurisdiction: Denver International Airport (DIA) to the Kansas Border*

While the Front Range battles urban density, the **I-70 East Corridor** leverages its flat, expansive prairie ROW to create a **250 ft wide** multi-modal super-artery. It separates high-speed intercity passengers from agricultural commuter rail while permanently removing heavy transcontinental freight from the general traffic lanes.

## 2. The 250-Foot Plains Cross-Section
Because land acquisition on the eastern plains is vastly less complex, we utilize a wide, flat cross-section designed to prevent snowdrift accumulation and maximize high-speed stability.

### Space Allocation (Center to Outer Edge)
* **High-Speed Rail Median (50 ft):** A fully fenced, depressed center median housing dual-track, electrified rail. The slight depression prevents crosswinds from destabilizing trains.
* **Median Safety Barrier (4 ft):** Continuous TL-6 rated concrete barriers.
* **General Purpose Lanes (24 ft per side):** Two **12 ft** lanes in each direction dedicated solely to passenger vehicles running at **75 mph**.
* **Automated Freight Platoon Lane (12 ft per side):** The far-right lane is restricted to heavy commercial and agricultural trucks utilizing Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) automation to platoon.
* **Agri-Commuter Local Rail (15 ft per side):** A single-track, bi-directional local rail line on the outer flank for regional transit.
* **Environmental Buffer / Snow Fences (20+ ft per side):** Graded drainage ditches and dense, tiered tree plantings.

## 3. Eastern Transit Operations
* **The Trans-Plains Express (High-Speed Rail):** Running at **150–220 mph**, this line connects Denver Union Station ➔ DIA Hub ➔ Limon ➔ Burlington ➔ Kansas City. It competes directly with regional air travel.
* **The High Plains Local (Agri-Commuter):** Running at **60–75 mph**, this train serves rural hubs (Strasburg, Byers, Limon, Flagler, Burlington), allowing agricultural workers and rural residents to commute to the Denver metro regardless of winter weather.

## 4. The Limon Multi-Modal Hub & Grid Integration
* **Freight Transfer Terminals:** Limon acts as an inland intermodal port where local agricultural goods from US-287/US-24 are loaded directly onto high-capacity freight trains.
* **The HVDC Energy Spine:** High-Voltage Direct Current transmission lines are buried beneath the High-Speed Rail median, piping clean wind energy directly from the plains into the Denver power grid.
* **Living Snow Fences:** Staggered rows of drought-resistant native conifers trap drifting snow, preventing whiteout conditions on the highway and tracks.

# PART II: iBetter 70 West (The Mountain & Western Slope Corridor)
*Jurisdiction: Golden (Mount Vernon Canyon) to Grand Junction*

A continuous quad-track rail system is geographically impossible here. To achieve maximum multi-modal throughput across the Continental Divide, this segment utilizes a dual-pronged strategy: **The Amtrak Zephyr Advanced** and the **iBetter BRT System**. Once the corridor clears the canyons, dedicated commuter rail is reintroduced.

## 5. Western Core Transit Modes
### A. The Intercity Express: "Zephyr Advanced"
* **Technology:** Active Tilt Trains (Pendolinos) capable of leaning into curves, allowing canyon navigation 25%–30% faster than traditional rolling stock.
* **Route:** Denver Union Station ➔ Winter Park ➔ Glenwood Springs ➔ Grand Junction.
* **Infrastructure Upgrades:** Passing sidings constructed at strategic intervals allow the Express to overtake slow-moving UP freight trains.

### B. The Mountain Local: "iBetter BRT" (Bustang Advanced)
* **Technology:** Heavy-duty, high-capacity articulated coaches equipped with automated drop-down chains, V2V platooning capabilities, and 7% grade hill-climb engineering.
* **Infrastructure:** A dedicated, reversible managed lane in the center median of I-70, protected by movable concrete zipper barriers. Westbound flow on Fridays, Eastbound on Sundays.

## 6. Route Walkthrough (East to West)

### Zone 1: The Foothills Climb (Golden to Empire)
* **The Challenge & Solution:** Congestion at Floyd Hill is bypassed via the **Reversible Managed BRT Lane**. Bustang coaches and HOV 3+ vehicles use this lane for free; single-occupancy vehicles pay a dynamic toll to access it, ensuring speeds remain above 55 mph.

### Zone 2: The Continental Divide (Eisenhower-Johnson Tunnel)
* **The Challenge & Solution:** Widening the 11,158-foot elevation tunnel is unfeasible. The BRT system is granted priority electronic signaling to bypass standard traffic metering lights at the tunnel portals.

### Zone 3: Vail Pass & Summit County
* **The Challenge & Solution:** Severe avalanche risk and wildlife crossings. The BRT lane features automated roadway heating. Landscaped wildlife overpasses direct herds away from traffic. BRT stations (Frisco, Vail) are built off-highway via dedicated slip-ramps.

### Zone 4: The Glenwood Canyon Pinch Point
* **The Challenge & Solution:** The highway is suspended over the river with zero expansion width. The "Zephyr Advanced" completely bypasses the highway layout. The local BRT relies on absolute priority signaling and rapid incident-clearing teams to navigate the existing viaducts.

### Zone 5: The Western Slope & Grand Junction Terminus
* **The Challenge & Solution:** Exiting De Beque Canyon, the ROW opens up. We transition back to a dedicated **Dual-Track Local Commuter Rail** running parallel to I-70. 
* **The Grand Junction Hub:** The rail hub links the downtown corridor directly with Colorado Mesa University (CMU), creating a traffic-free, high-frequency transit lifeline connecting CMU students, Palisade agricultural workers, and commuters straight through to Rifle and Glenwood Springs.

## 7. Mountain Engineering & Safety Hardening
* **Off-Highway Hubs:** Bus stops are strictly forbidden on the highway shoulder. All BRT transfers occur at dedicated, weather-proof transit centers.
* **Avalanche Shelters:** Extended concrete snow-sheds are constructed over the highway and rail lines in known avalanche paths (e.g., Seven Sisters near Loveland Pass) to prevent corridor closures.
