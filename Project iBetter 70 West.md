# Project iBetter 70 West: The Mountain & Western Slope Corridor
**Document Version:** 1.0.2 (Final Draft)
**Lead Architect:** Chief Engineer Flench
**Jurisdiction:** Colorado Transit Authority & Western Slope Division

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Unlike the wide-open plains of iBetter 70 East or the urban expanses of iBetter 25, the **iBetter 70 West** corridor requires a surgical, hybrid infrastructure approach. Constrained by 7% mountain grades, narrow blast-rock canyons, and extreme alpine weather, a continuous quad-track rail system is geographically impossible. 

To achieve maximum multi-modal throughput across the Continental Divide, this master plan utilizes a dual-pronged strategy: **The Amtrak Zephyr Advanced** (high-speed intercity tilt trains operating on existing heavy rail alignments) and the **iBetter BRT System** (advanced Bus Rapid Transit utilizing a reversible, automated managed lane on the I-70 highway itself). Once the corridor clears the canyons and enters the Grand Valley, dedicated commuter rail is reintroduced.

---

## 2. Core Transit Modes

### A. The Intercity Express: "Zephyr Advanced"
* **Technology:** Active Tilt Trains (Pendolinos) capable of leaning into curves, allowing canyon navigation 25%–30% faster than traditional rolling stock.
* **Route:** Denver Union Station ➔ Winter Park ➔ Glenwood Springs ➔ Grand Junction.
* **Infrastructure Upgrades:** Passing sidings constructed at strategic intervals to allow the Express to overtake slow-moving Union Pacific freight trains without losing speed.

### B. The Mountain Local: "iBetter BRT" (Bustang Advanced)
* **Technology:** Heavy-duty, high-capacity articulated coaches equipped with automated drop-down chains, Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) platooning capabilities, and 7% grade hill-climb engineering.
* **Infrastructure:** A dedicated, reversible managed lane in the center median of I-70 (where space permits), protected by movable concrete zipper barriers. Westbound flow on Fridays, Eastbound on Sundays.

## 3. Route Walkthrough (East to West)

### Zone 1: The Foothills Climb (Golden to Empire)
* **Terrain Challenge:** Immediate congestion and steep climbs starting at Mount Vernon Canyon and Floyd Hill.
* **The Solution:** The physical iBetter 70 **Reversible Managed BRT Lane** begins here. During peak weekend traffic, the zipper barrier deploys. Bustang coaches and HOV 3+ vehicles use this lane for free, bypassing miles of gridlock, while single-occupancy vehicles pay a dynamic toll to access it, ensuring the lane never drops below 55 mph.

### Zone 2: The Continental Divide (Eisenhower-Johnson Tunnel)
* **Terrain Challenge:** The tunnel sits at 11,158 feet—the highest point on the Interstate Highway System. Widening the tunnel bore for new transit is economically unfeasible.
* **The Solution:** The BRT system is granted priority electronic signaling to bypass the standard traffic metering lights at the tunnel portals, allowing coaches to seamlessly merge into the tunnel without waiting in the miles-long queues.

### Zone 3: Vail Pass & Summit County
* **Terrain Challenge:** 10,666-foot elevation, severe avalanche risk, and high wildlife crossing volume.
* **The Solution:** iBetter 70 introduces automated roadway heating systems in the BRT lane to prevent ice accumulation. Landscaped wildlife overpasses (green bridges) are constructed over the highway, directing migrating herds away from high-speed traffic and transit lanes. Off-highway BRT stations are built at the Frisco and Vail transit centers via dedicated slip-ramps.

### Zone 4: The Glenwood Canyon Pinch Point
* **Terrain Challenge:** The highway is a cantilevered engineering marvel suspended over the Colorado River or clinging to the canyon wall. Zero expansion width available.
* **The Solution:** The "Zephyr Advanced" tilting trains completely bypass the highway layout on the opposite side of the river. Meanwhile, the local mountain BRT relies on absolute priority signaling and rapid incident-clearing teams to navigate the existing suspended viaducts without delay.

### Zone 5: The Western Slope & Grand Junction Terminus
* **Terrain Challenge:** Transitioning from canyon constraints to an open, sprawling valley.
* **The Solution:** Exiting De Beque Canyon, the Right-of-Way dramatically opens up. Here, we transition back to a dedicated **Dual-Track Local Commuter Rail** running parallel to I-70. 
* **The Grand Junction Hub:** Grand Junction serves as the ultimate Western terminus. The rail hub is placed strategically to link the downtown corridor directly with Colorado Mesa University (CMU). This creates a traffic-free, high-frequency transit lifeline connecting CMU students, Palisade agricultural workers, and Grand Junction commuters straight up through Rifle and Glenwood Springs.

---

## 4. Engineering & Safety Hardening

* **Off-Highway Hubs:** Bus stops are strictly forbidden on the highway shoulder. All BRT transfers occur at dedicated, weather-proof transit centers (Idaho Springs, Frisco, Vail) accessed via dedicated slip ramps so buses never re-enter gridlocked general traffic to merge.
* **Avalanche Shelters:** Extended concrete snow-sheds constructed over the highway and rail lines in known avalanche paths (like the Seven Sisters near Loveland Pass) to prevent corridor closures during extreme winter loading.
